# Great Demo Glossary

Definitions of Cohan's terms. Use this when the user asks what a term means or when a precise definition is needed in output.

**Benefit**: the gain or advantage provided to the customer through use of a Specific Capability.

**Booth Shark**: person staffing a trade show or conference booth, responsible for engaging and initially qualifying prospects.

**Canned Demo**: pre-prepared Generic Demo, typically recorded, used when a live demo can't be delivered. Same as Recorded Demo.

**Capability**: a feature offered by a software package. By itself, of little or no value. Value comes from addressing a Reason behind a CBI.

**CBI (Critical Business Issue)**: the key problem a customer must solve to achieve a major objective. Stated in the customer's own terms.

**Chain of Pain**: the interrelationship between customer players' CBIs, Reasons, and Specific Capabilities across the org chart. Senior players' Reasons become junior players' CBIs.

**Champion**: a customer representative who actively supports the purchase and implementation of your capabilities. Provides access to key people and resources within the customer organization.

**Cosmetic Bug**: a defect that doesn't impact functionality; affects only what's seen on screen.

**Database Breakeven Point**: the point at which a database contains enough content to justify its use to a typical user.

**Delta**: the measurable difference between the customer's current process and the future process with your solution. Expressed in money, time, or people. Four types: Cheaper, Better, Faster, Couldn't Be Done Before.

**Demo Days**: focused, mutual demonstration sessions put on by and for the SE organization. Used for skill development and knowledge sharing.

**Demonstration**: a presentation of the set of Specific Capabilities needed to solve a customer's CBI.

**Demonstration Roadmap**: a slide or set of slides identifying the overall pathway and current section of a complex demo. Same content as the Outline; rephrased for the audience. Used heavily in Remote Demos to maintain orientation.

**Direct Research**: gathering information through direct contact with the customer.

**"Do It"**: the most concise, rational pathway from the start of a demonstration to the Illustration. The fewest mouse clicks required to reach the Illustration screen.

**"Do It Again"**: a second pass through the same pathway as the Do It, with more detail and richer explanation.

**Earned Reputation**: the trust, demonstrated competence, and efficacy a person achieves in the minds of customers. Built by handling customer information well over time.

**Elevator Demo**: a concise, clear description of the solutions and applications your product enables. The best ones include one or more Illustrations as a take-away.

**Evaluation**: hands-on technical proof, performed by the customer using the actual software. Out of scope for this skill.

**Feature**: a capability available in a software package. Same as Capability for these purposes; has no value standalone.

**Generic Demo**: a demonstration given to an unqualified audience.

**Good Question**: a question asked during a demo that is meaningful but not directly relevant to what's currently on screen. Defer to the Not Now List.

**Great Demo**: the managed, effective process of presenting the Specific Capabilities a customer needs.

**Great Question**: a question that supports or reinforces the value of a Specific Capability, or that helps the demonstration flow forward. Answer right away, briefly.

**Illustration**: a concise, visual artifact that communicates the reality or proof of a Solution. The "wow" screen. Shown first in the demo.

**Indirect Research**: gathering information through channels that don't involve direct customer contact: papers, publications, websites, patents, product documentation.

**Infrastructure Checklist**: list of all hardware, software, network, audio-visual, and facilities requirements for a demonstration.

**Interdependency**: a linkage between people or events such that one or more people's actions positively or negatively impact others in the same organization.

**Introduction**: provides the audience with the outline of the demo. The demonstration agenda, read aloud.

**Not Now List**: a method of queuing and organizing audience questions during a demo. Gives the demonstrator control over questions, answers, pace, and audience attention.

**Outline**: defines the presence and order of the building blocks of a demo. Provides the basic text for the Introduction.

**Project**: a specific sales process, more accurate than "deal." Has identified players, project management parameters, a beginning, and an end.

**Qualification**: the process of identifying CBIs, Reasons, and Specific Capabilities for a particular customer.

**Reason**: the sub-problem(s) responsible for a customer's inability to solve their CBI.

**Recorded Demo**: same as Canned Demo.

**Reference Story**: a Situation Slide drawn from a past success, used to generate hope and curiosity in Vision Generation demos and Qualification meetings. Usually sanitized to protect the original customer's confidentiality.

**Remote Demo**: a demonstration where the audience is physically separated from the presenter, delivered via collaboration tools.

**Rollover Agreement**: an evaluation license agreement that presupposes successful completion. Streamlines the legal process at close. Out of scope for this skill.

**Sales Methodology**: a process for managing and executing sales. Examples include CustomerCentric Selling, Solution Selling, VITO, SPIN, and Miller-Heiman.

**Salesperson**: the Selling Team member responsible for identifying customer CBIs, Reasons, and Specific Capabilities, and for organizing the method, objectives, and timing of the demo.

**SE (Sales Engineer)**: the technical resource expected to be fluent in the capabilities and operating environments of a company's products. Owns demo construction and delivery.

**Selling Team**: typically a Salesperson and an SE; more complex compositions for more complex sales situations.

**Sequence of Events**: a tool for evaluations and other multi-step sales processes that organizes and orchestrates players, tasks, timing, and responsibilities. Out of scope for this skill.

**Serious Bug**: a defect that impacts functionality enough to make the software unusable for the affected task.

**Situation Slide**: a slide presented immediately before the Illustration. For Technical Proof: contains Company, Job Title, CBI, Reason, Specific Capabilities, and Delta from Qualification. For Vision Generation: may be a Reference Story.

**Solution**: the set of Specific Capabilities provided by your company, in context, that solves a customer's CBI. May span products and services.

**Specific Capability**: a capability required to address or solve a customer's CBI. The third element in the CBI/Reason/Specific Capability chain.

**Stupid Question**: a question that should not have been asked. Two sources: hostile audience members and audience members who genuinely don't understand. Handle on the Not Now List with full respect, same as a Good Question.

**Tips and Tricks Session**: a problem-solving and "how to" session held at a customer site post-sale. Format: 1-2 hours, handful of users, real problems. Builds trust and surfaces feedback that improves future demos.

**To Do List**: a method of queuing and organizing capabilities and Specific Capabilities desired by an audience. Used on the fly to identify and list audience needs. Particularly useful for unexpectedly large or unqualified audiences.

**Workflow**: a series of interdependent steps or sub-processes executed by members of a customer's organization to achieve a specific outcome.
