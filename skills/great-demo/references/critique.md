# Demo Critique

How to score an existing demo script against the Great Demo! method.

## How to Use This Rubric

The user has supplied a demo script (theirs, a colleague's, a draft from another tool). Run through the rubric below. For each item, decide pass, fail, or partial. Cite specific lines or sections from the script. End with a prioritized list of fixes.

Don't soften failures. The whole point of a critique is to catch the things that will lose the deal.

If the script is missing prerequisite context (no audience, no CBI, no time budget), surface that first. A script that looks fine on paper can still be wrong for the actual room.

## The Rubric

### 1. Does it Do The Last Thing First?

Look at the first three minutes of the script. Is the strongest result on screen by minute two? Or does the script open with a login, a corporate overview, a navigation tour, or a feature catalog?

- **Pass**: Illustration appears within the first two minutes.
- **Partial**: Some setup (Situation Slide, brief intro) precedes the Illustration but is short and tied to the customer's situation.
- **Fail**: Audience waits more than three minutes before seeing the wow result. This is the most common failure mode in real-world demos.

If failed: the fix is structural. The Illustration moves up. Everything else reorders around it.

### 2. Is there a Situation Slide before the Illustration?

The Situation Slide re-establishes who the customer is, what they're trying to solve, and what the Delta looks like. Without it, the Illustration lacks context.

- **Pass**: Situation Slide includes job title, CBI, Reason(s), Specific Capabilities, and a quantified Delta.
- **Partial**: Some elements present but Delta is unquantified or CBI is generic.
- **Fail**: No Situation Slide, or the slide is just a corporate intro.

### 3. Does every Specific Capability tie to a stated CBI?

Walk through the script and list every capability shown. For each one, ask: which CBI does this address? If you can't answer for a capability, it's a feature parade item.

- **Pass**: Every capability traces to a named CBI for a named stakeholder.
- **Partial**: Most capabilities tied to CBIs; a few unjustified additions.
- **Fail**: Capabilities shown with no CBI behind them, or "because the product does it."

If failed: name the unjustified capabilities. Recommend cuts.

### 4. Is the Do It actually short?

Count the clicks in the Do It section. Time the description. The target is one to two minutes.

- **Pass**: Do It under two minutes, fewest possible clicks, no side explanations.
- **Partial**: Do It around three to five minutes; some unnecessary side trips.
- **Fail**: Do It exceeds five minutes, or is indistinguishable from the Do It Again.

### 5. Does the Do It Again stay focused?

The Do It Again is the scenic route through the same pathway. It should not branch into unrelated features.

- **Pass**: Do It Again expands on the Specific Capabilities only.
- **Partial**: Mostly focused with one or two off-topic detours.
- **Fail**: Tour of unrelated capabilities. The deal-killer Cohan describes: a $250K order shrunk to $5K because the SE turned a 10-minute demo into a two-hour feature tour.

### 6. Is the Delta quantified?

Search the script for numbers. Time savings, headcount, dollars, error rates.

- **Pass**: Delta expressed in money, time, or people, with the customer's own numbers if possible.
- **Partial**: Some numbers, but vague ("much faster," "significant savings").
- **Fail**: No Delta, or Delta in feature terms ("provides comprehensive workflow automation").

If failed: surface the missing numbers. Suggest the discovery questions that would extract them ("How long does your process take today?", "What do you need this to become?").

### 7. Are buzzwords present?

Scan for the Content-Free Buzzword-Compliant list:

robust, powerful, flexible, integrated, seamless, extensible, scalable, interoperable, easy-to-use, intuitive, user-friendly, comprehensive, best-of-breed, world-class, cutting-edge

For each one found: name the line, propose a concrete replacement.

- **Pass**: No buzzwords. Concrete claims throughout.
- **Partial**: One or two buzzwords; quick fix.
- **Fail**: Buzzword-dense. Credibility risk.

Test for a borderline phrase: ask "in whose opinion?" If the answer is Marketing or "us," rephrase with concrete facts.

### 8. Does each segment end on the strongest Illustration screen?

Look at what the audience sees at the end of each segment. The last image is the one they take home.

- **Pass**: Every segment closes on the best Illustration available.
- **Partial**: Most segments do; one or two close on a generic screen.
- **Fail**: Segments end on settings pages, default views, or transition screens.

### 9. Is question handling planned?

Look for explicit Not Now List handling, Q&A placement, and pre-prepared responses for likely questions.

- **Pass**: Not Now List approach noted, Q&A is positioned after the final Do It Again summary, common questions anticipated.
- **Partial**: Q&A present but no Not Now List discipline.
- **Fail**: Questions handled inline as they come up. High risk of side-discussions and lost control.

### 10. Are file names realistic?

Check any file names referenced in the script.

- **Pass**: Files named with the customer's company or relevant business context.
- **Partial**: Some realistic names, some generic.
- **Fail**: "demo1," "test," "Untitled," "DEMO_FINAL_v3."

### 11. Are keyboard shortcuts used?

Search for "Ctrl+", "Cmd+", "shortcut," or any non-mouse navigation.

- **Pass**: All actions go through visible menus, buttons, or clicks.
- **Partial**: Mostly visible; one or two shortcuts that don't matter.
- **Fail**: Shortcuts used during key Do It moments. Audience can't envision themselves doing it.

### 12. Does the timing add up?

Sum the segment times. Compare to total budget.

- **Pass**: Total within budget, with a buffer for Q&A.
- **Partial**: Total at or slightly over budget.
- **Fail**: Total exceeds budget, especially over 30 minutes for a single solution. Cohan's data: long demos lose deals more often than short ones.

### 13. Is there a real Summary at the end?

The final Summary recaps each Specific Capability and its tie to the customer's CBI. It is not a generic "thanks for your time."

- **Pass**: Summary names each Specific Capability shown and the CBI it addresses.
- **Partial**: Summary present but generic.
- **Fail**: No summary, or a thank-you with no recap.

## Output Format

Produce a critique in this structure:

1. **One-line verdict**: pass, needs work, or fundamental rework
2. **Top three fixes** in priority order, each one specific to the script and citing a line or section
3. **Full rubric scoring**: each item with pass/partial/fail and a sentence of evidence
4. **Recommended rewrites** for the highest-impact failures: specific replacement language or structural changes

Don't list every minor issue. Prioritize. The user can fix three big things and ship; they can't fix thirty.

## What This Rubric Does Not Cover

- Brand voice, language style, or tone-of-voice questions specific to a company. Use a brand-specific skill (like `bluedolphin-collateral`) for those.
- Slide design, layout, typography. Out of scope.
- Audience-specific psychological adjustments beyond CBI/Reason mapping. Cohan's method assumes the qualification work was done correctly; if you suspect it wasn't, surface that gap and run a Qualify pass first.
- Evaluation management or Sequence of Events work. Out of scope.

## Worked Example

Script handed in: "Open with a 5-minute corporate overview slide deck. Then login to the system. Walk through each module: Dashboard, Reporting, Admin, User Management. Show all features in each module. End with a thank-you slide."

Critique output:

**Verdict**: fundamental rework.

**Top three fixes**:

1. Cut the corporate overview to under one minute or remove it. The audience is here for the demo, not the company history. Move the Illustration into the first two minutes.
2. Stop the module tour. Pick the one screen that addresses the customer's CBI and lead with it as the Illustration. Build a Do It path to it. Build a Do It Again that explores depth within that one capability.
3. Add a Situation Slide before the Illustration. Without it, the Illustration has no context. Without the CBI/Reason/Specific Capability anchors, the demo is a feature parade.

**Rubric scoring**:

1. Do The Last Thing First: fail. Audience waits five-plus minutes for any product visual.
2. Situation Slide: fail. None present.
3. Specific Capabilities tied to CBI: fail. No CBI mentioned anywhere; module tour implies all features are equally relevant.
4. Do It length: fail. The "module tour" is the entire demo; there is no fast pathway.
5. Do It Again focus: not applicable; no separation between fast and detailed passes.
6. Quantified Delta: fail. No numbers.
7. Buzzwords: cannot assess without full script text; flag for review.
8. Strongest Illustration screen at segment end: fail. Demo ends on a thank-you slide.
9. Question handling: fail. No Not Now List discipline noted.
10. File names: cannot assess.
11. Keyboard shortcuts: cannot assess.
12. Timing: cannot assess until structure is fixed.
13. Real Summary: fail. Thank-you slide is not a Specific-Capability recap.

**Recommended rewrites**: replace the opening five minutes with: 30-second introduction reading the outline, two-minute Situation Slide tied to the customer's actual CBI, two-minute Illustration showing the outcome they care about most. Then a four-minute Do It. Then a Do It Again of seven to ten minutes covering the Specific Capabilities. Then Q&A using the Not Now List for deferred questions. Then a Summary that names each Specific Capability shown and its tie to the CBI.
