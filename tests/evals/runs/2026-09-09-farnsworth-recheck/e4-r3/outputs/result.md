**Final Text:**

We run four separate customer data platforms from acquisitions between 2021 and 2024. They cost us $2.1M annually in duplicated licensing alone, and we can't see our customer data as one picture across the business.

Consolidating to one platform would take eighteen months. We'd need dedicated engineering from three teams and close coordination with sales to maintain customer reporting during the transition. Licensing savings would offset implementation costs by month twenty-six. We'd also see faster reporting and better data for analytics, though these gains are harder to measure precisely.

Real risks exist. The migration touches billing systems while finance implements new revenue recognition. Timing is critical here. Two of the four platforms lack documentation, which complicates effort estimation. The teams we'd assign are already committed to regulatory reporting; we haven't confirmed they can carry both in parallel.

We also must coordinate customer communications. Any migration affecting customer reporting requires advance notice to affected accounts. Sales has flagged the volume of change notifications sent to key accounts this year, so we'll need coordination on timing.

On platform selection: preliminary analysis suggests two are technically viable. Two have real limits on data model extensibility and integration tooling. We haven't agreed on evaluation criteria with stakeholders. The largest platform's vendor has offered enterprise pricing if we consolidate onto their system—this shouldn't be read as our recommendation.

What does this ask require? Approval to proceed with detailed planning. Not approval to build; approval to plan. This planning phase has a clear decision gate before any engineering commitment. We cannot answer whether consolidation is worth the risk without planning. Planning costs time and focused effort. It gives us the facts to decide. We ask for approval to plan.
