Executive Summary: Platform Consolidation Initiative

Why do we have four platforms? Acquisitions in 2021 and 2024 brought them. Now they create two problems: approximately $2.1M annually in duplicate licensing, and no unified customer reporting.

The consolidation initiative would move everything to one platform in eighteen months. We'd need dedicated resources from three teams, plus close coordination with sales to keep customer operations continuous during transition.

The math works: licensing savings offset implementation costs by month twenty-six. Additional upside—faster reporting cycles and better data quality for analytics—is harder to quantify but possible.

We face material risks. The migration would touch billing systems. Finance is also implementing new revenue recognition at the same time; sequencing is critical. Two of the four platforms lack comprehensive documentation, making effort estimation uncertain. And the teams we'd assign are already committed to regulatory reporting; we haven't fully determined whether both efforts fit in parallel.

On platform selection: the largest provider has indicated willingness to discuss enterprise pricing, but that isn't a recommendation. Platform selection hasn't started, and evaluation criteria haven't been agreed with stakeholders. Preliminary indications suggest two would be technically viable as targets. The other two present substantial limitations in data model extensibility and integration tooling maturity.

Customer communication needs haven't been scoped. Any migration affecting customer-facing reporting will require advance notification to affected accounts. The commercial organization has expressed concerns about the volume of change notifications to strategic accounts during the current year. Account management coordination will be essential before we schedule.

Approve the planning phase. We'll return with a decision point before any committed engineering investment.
