**Executive Summary: Platform Consolidation Initiative**

We run four separate customer data platforms, inherited through acquisitions made between 2021 and 2024. The fragmentation costs us at least two things: roughly $2.1M a year in duplicated licensing, and the ability to produce unified customer reporting across business units.

The proposal is to migrate all customer data onto a single platform over eighteen months. That would take dedicated engineers from three separate teams, and substantial coordination with the commercial organization to keep customer-facing operations running while the data moves.

Financial modelling indicates that licensing savings would offset the implementation cost by month twenty-six. The other benefits are harder to quantify with precision, but could include faster reporting cycles and better data quality for the analytics functions downstream.

The risks are material. The migration would touch billing systems while the finance organization is implementing a new revenue recognition process, so sequencing is critical. Separately, two of the four platforms are not fully documented, which makes the work hard to size.

Capacity is a further concern. The engineering teams identified for this work are also committed to the regulatory reporting programme, and we have not yet fully assessed how much of both they can carry at once.

We are asking leadership to approve a detailed planning phase, with a decision gate at the end of that phase before any engineering investment is committed. This is a request to plan, not a request to build.

The incumbent provider of the largest of the four platforms has signalled it is willing to discuss enterprise pricing if consolidation proceeds onto their platform. That is not a recommendation. Platform selection has not yet begun, and the evaluation criteria have not been agreed with stakeholders. Early indications suggest that two of the four platforms would be technically viable as consolidation targets. The other two have substantial limitations: data models that are hard to extend, and integration tooling that is not yet mature.

Customer communication requirements have not yet been scoped. Any migration that affects customer-facing reporting will require advance notice to the affected accounts, and the commercial organization has already raised concerns about the number of change notices sent to strategic accounts this fiscal year. Any schedule will therefore have to go through account management first.
