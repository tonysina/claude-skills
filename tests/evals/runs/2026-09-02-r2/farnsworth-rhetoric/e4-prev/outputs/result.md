**Executive Summary: Platform Consolidation Initiative**

We run four separate customer data platforms, inherited through acquisitions between 2021 and 2024. The fragmentation causes significant operational inefficiency. We cannot produce unified customer reporting across business units, and duplicated licensing alone runs to roughly $2.1M a year.

The proposed consolidation would move all customer data onto one platform over eighteen months. It would need dedicated engineers from three separate teams, and substantial coordination with the commercial organization to keep customer-facing operations running while the data moves.

Financial modelling indicates that licensing savings would offset implementation costs by month twenty-six. Other benefits are harder to put a number on. They could include faster reporting cycles and better data quality for the analytics work downstream.

The risks are material. The migration would touch billing systems while finance is rolling out a new revenue recognition process, so sequencing is critical. Two of the four platforms also lack comprehensive documentation, which makes the effort harder to size.

Capacity is a further concern. The engineering teams identified for this work are also committed to the regulatory reporting programme, and we have not yet fully assessed how much of both they can carry at once.

We are asking leadership to approve a detailed planning phase. What does approval commit us to? Planning, and no more. A decision gate at the end of that phase sits before any committed engineering spend.

On vendors: the incumbent provider of the largest of the four platforms has said it is willing to discuss enterprise pricing if consolidation proceeds onto its platform. That is not a recommendation. We have not yet selected a platform, and stakeholders have not yet agreed the evaluation criteria. Preliminary indications suggest two of the four platforms would be technically viable as consolidation targets; the other two have substantial limits in data model extensibility and in the maturity of their integration tools.

Finally, customer communication has not yet been scoped. Any migration that touches customer-facing reporting will require advance notice to the affected accounts, and the commercial organization has already raised concerns about the volume of change notifications sent to strategic accounts this fiscal year. We will coordinate with account management before we set any dates.
