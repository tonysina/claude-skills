Here's the humanized version. I kept the professional register and the one-pager shape (opener, what it does, benefit list, the honest caveat).

---

Onboarding is where a customer forms their first real opinion of your product. It's also where many of them give up.

Our onboarding platform is built so new users can get from signup to a working setup on their own. It connects to the systems you already run, and most teams finish setup in minutes rather than hours.

The platform also records where users stall or abandon the flow, step by step. That tells you which screen to fix, instead of leaving you to guess at the funnel as a whole.

For the user, it comes down to three things:

- Setup takes minutes, not hours.
- Every step says what it needs and why.
- A progress marker shows what's done and what's left.

The hard part is legacy systems. Integrations with older internal tools take longer than the rest, and our support engineers work through those with your team during implementation.

---

## What I changed

The draft scored well past the rewrite threshold: roughly nine watch-list hits per 100 words across eight distinct patterns, plus three constructions that count on their own regardless of density.

**Significance inflation (`INFLATION`).** "rapidly evolving digital landscape," "pivotal moment in the customer journey," "stands as a testament to our commitment to excellence." Four separate claims that onboarding is important, none of them saying anything about your product. Replaced with the one concrete reason it matters: people quit there.

**Copula avoidance (`NO-COPULA`).** "serves as a pivotal moment," "stands as a testament." Both are "is" wearing a costume.

**Negative parallelism (`NEG-PARALLEL`).** "It's not just a form to fill out; it's the first real impression" and "onboarding is more than just a process. It's the foundation of lasting customer relationships." Two instances of correcting a misconception the reader never had. I kept the actual point of the first one (onboarding is the first impression) and cut the second, which only repeated it.

**AI vocabulary (`AI-VOCAB`).** "Additionally" opening a sentence, "leverages," "robust," "enhance," "fostering," "seamless," "showcasing." A cluster this dense is one of the strongest tells in the list.

**Superficial -ing analysis (`ING-ANALYSIS`).** "showcasing intuitive design," "fostering a culture of continuous improvement across the organization." Participle phrases tacked on to sound like analysis. The analytics paragraph now says what the analytics actually do.

**Vague attribution (`VAGUE-ATTRIB`).** "Industry reports indicate that organizations with streamlined onboarding see significantly higher retention rates." No report is named and "significantly" is doing unearned work. I cut it rather than invent a source. If you have a real number, from a named study or your own customer data, that sentence is worth rebuilding around the number.

**Bolded inline-header list (`BOLD-LISTS`).** "**Speed:** / **Clarity:** / **Confidence:**" is the single most recognizable ChatGPT format. The three items are genuinely distinct, so I kept them as bullets and dropped the alliterative headers. "Confidence" was the weakest of the three, since it restated Clarity as a feeling, so it became the progress marker that actually produces the feeling.

**Challenges formula (`CHALLENGES-FORMULA`).** "Despite these advantages, some challenges remain... However, with our dedicated support team and ongoing product investment, the road ahead is promising." The formula neutralizes the admission it just made. I kept the admission, since naming legacy integration as the hard part reads as credible in a sales document, and replaced the reassurance with what your team actually does about it.

**Generic closers and didactic wrap-up (`GENERIC-CLOSER`, `DIDACTIC`).** "the road ahead is promising," "In conclusion," "The future looks bright." Three closing moves in four sentences, none carrying information. Cut.

## Two things worth your attention

The rewrite is deliberately thinner on claims than the original, because most of what I removed was assertion rather than fact. Two places want your real numbers before this ships:

1. **"Minutes rather than hours."** This came from the original draft. If it's measured, say from what to what ("median setup is 8 minutes, down from about 40 in the legacy flow"), and it becomes the strongest line on the page. If it isn't measured, it's a claim a buyer can test on day one.
2. **The retention statistic I removed.** Worth restoring with a real source attached.

I also stated the integration point plainly rather than specifically, since I don't know which systems you connect to. Naming two or three real ones would do more for this section than any wording change.
