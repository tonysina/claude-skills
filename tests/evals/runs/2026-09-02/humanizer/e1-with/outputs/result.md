Here is the rewritten opening. It keeps the professional register and every claim from the original, but drops the AI patterns.

Onboarding is the first thing a new customer does with your product, and it sets the tone for everything that follows. If setup is slow or confusing, that is the impression they walk away with.

Our onboarding platform is built to make that first experience a good one. Setup takes minutes rather than hours. Each step comes with guidance in context, so users know what a field means and what comes next. They can see where they are in the process and what is left to do. The platform connects to the systems you already run.

It also tracks how users move through setup, so your team can see where people slow down or drop off and fix those steps. Companies that shorten onboarding tend to keep more of the customers they sign.

Connecting older or custom-built systems takes more work than connecting modern ones. Our support team works through those integrations with you, and legacy connectivity is an active area of product investment.

Onboarding is where a customer relationship starts. We built this platform to get that start right.

---

**What changed and why**

The original scored well past the rewrite threshold: about 13 watch-list hits in 171 words (roughly 7.6 per 100) across five patterns, plus several constructions that are findings on their own. Patterns fixed, by ID:

- `INFLATION` -- "rapidly evolving digital landscape," "pivotal moment," "stands as a testament to our commitment to excellence," "lasting." Replaced with plain statements of what the platform does.
- `AI-VOCAB` -- "Additionally," "robust," "enhance," "showcasing," "fostering," "key," "landscape," "pivotal," "testament." All removed.
- `ING-ANALYSIS` -- "showcasing intuitive design...," "fostering a culture of continuous improvement." Converted to sentences with a subject and a verb.
- `NO-COPULA` -- "serves as a pivotal moment," "stands as a testament." Now "is."
- `VAGUE-ATTRIB` -- "Industry reports indicate that organizations with streamlined onboarding see significantly higher retention rates." Rewritten as a direct claim without the phantom source. See the note below.
- `NEG-PARALLEL` -- "It's not just a form to fill out; it's..." and "onboarding is more than just a process. It's..." Both restructured so the point stands without the correction-of-a-misconception frame.
- `BOLD-LISTS` -- the three bolded inline-header bullets (Speed, Clarity, Confidence) are now prose in the second paragraph. The three facts survived; the labels did not.
- `CHALLENGES-FORMULA` -- "Despite these advantages, some challenges remain... the road ahead is promising." Replaced with a plain statement of the actual limitation (legacy integration) and what you do about it.
- `GENERIC-CLOSER` -- "the road ahead is promising," "The future looks bright." Cut.
- `DIDACTIC` -- "In conclusion." Cut.

Two things to check before this ships:

1. The retention claim has no source in your draft. I kept it as a plain statement, but for a one-pager you will want either a named source with a figure or to cut the sentence.
2. "Intuitive design" and "seamless integration" were promotional adjectives with nothing behind them, so I replaced them with what the product does (contextual guidance, connects to existing systems). If you have a specific figure, such as a median setup time or a count of supported integrations, drop it into the second paragraph. Concrete numbers will do more for this opening than any adjective.

If the one-pager layout needs the benefits as bullets, the second paragraph splits back into three short lines without the bold labels.
