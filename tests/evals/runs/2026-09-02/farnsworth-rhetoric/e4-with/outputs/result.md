**Executive Summary: Platform Consolidation Initiative**

We maintain four separate customer data platforms and cannot produce one unified customer report across business units. The four came with acquisitions between 2021 and 2024. The duplicated licensing costs approximately $2.1M a year.

The proposal is to migrate all customer data to a single platform over eighteen months. That would take dedicated engineers from three separate teams, and close coordination with the commercial organization so that customer-facing operations keep running while data moves.

Financial modelling indicates that licensing savings would offset implementation costs by month twenty-six. Further benefits are possible but harder to quantify: faster reporting cycles, and cleaner data for the analytics work downstream.

The risks are material. The migration would touch billing systems while the finance organization is rolling out a new revenue recognition process, so sequencing is critical. Two of the four platforms also lack comprehensive documentation, so the size of that work is still in doubt.

Capacity is a further concern. The engineering teams identified for this initiative are already committed to the regulatory reporting programme, and we have not yet fully assessed how far they can carry both.

We are asking leadership to approve a detailed planning phase, with a decision gate at the end of it before any engineering investment is committed. Why a planning phase rather than a decision now? Because two questions are still open: how much work the two undocumented platforms will take, and whether the teams can carry it and the regulatory programme at once.

On vendors: the provider of the largest of the four platforms has said it is willing to discuss enterprise pricing if consolidation proceeds onto its platform. That is not a recommendation. No platform has been selected, and the evaluation criteria have not been agreed with stakeholders. Early indications suggest that two of the four platforms would be technically viable targets; the other two have substantial limitations: their data models are hard to extend and their integration tooling is immature.

Finally, customer communication has not yet been scoped. Any migration that affects customer-facing reporting will require advance notice to the accounts affected, and the commercial organization has already raised concerns about the number of change notifications strategic accounts have received this fiscal year. Account management must therefore be involved before any date is set.
