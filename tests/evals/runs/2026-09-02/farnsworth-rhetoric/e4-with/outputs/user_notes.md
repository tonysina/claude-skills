## Uncertainty
- Budget arithmetic. SKILL.md gives "half budget" for executive summaries and a cap of 3 for 300–600 words. Half of 3 is 1.5; I rounded up to 2 and spent both. A stricter reading would allow only 1, in which case the hypophora at the ask should stay and the antithesis at the open should go.
- Figure placement. The register table says "figures at the open and close only". The close of this piece is a customer-communication caveat, not the ask. I placed the second figure at the ask (paragraph 6 of 8) instead of the literal close, on the grounds that the rule's purpose is to put figures where attention is and where the piece must land. Reasonable, but it is a deviation from the letter of the table.
- The hypophora answer ("Because two questions are still open...") states a causal link the original never states. Both facts are in the original and the summary's structure implies the link, but the author may have a different reason for the planning-phase-only ask (budget cycle, board timing) that should replace mine.

## Needs Human Review

- "the size of that work is still in doubt" for "introduces uncertainty into effort estimation". Same claim in plainer words, but "in doubt" reads a touch more negative than "uncertainty". Author should confirm the tone is right for their leadership team.
- Dropped umbrella phrase "significant operational inefficiencies, including". If there are inefficiencies beyond the two named, the revision now under-claims.
- Dropped "with precision" from "difficult to quantify with precision". If rough figures for the secondary benefits exist, the original wording kept that door open and the revision closes it.

## Workarounds
- None. The skill's workflow was followed as written. `references/figures.md` was not loaded because none of the secondary figures had a trigger and the user did not name a figure.

## Suggestions
- The register table's "half budget" would be easier to apply if it gave a number per length band instead of a fraction of the length cap. "Executive summary: 1 figure under 300 words, 2 otherwise" would remove the rounding question.
- The rule "figures at the open and close only" assumes the ask is at the close. Executive summaries often bury the ask mid-document. A one-line note ("or at the ask, if the ask is not at the close") would cover the common case without loosening the constraint.
- The skill says it does not restructure, which is right, but a buried ask is the single most common reason an executive summary fails to land. The "Left alone" section is the correct place to flag it, and the skill could say so explicitly: "If the ask is not the last thing the reader sees, say so in Left alone and recommend the move."
