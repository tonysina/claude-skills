The original is 22 words, and almost all of them are category language: "comprehensive," "enabling," "strategic alignment," "integrated," "collaborative," "capabilities." Strip those away and the actual promise underneath is simple. Contoso lets people see their enterprise architecture and govern it together, so strategy and execution line up.

**Recommended**

> **Contoso: See your enterprise. Steer it together.**

Two short verbs, two parallel halves, and it keeps both real ideas from the original (visualization, shared governance) while implying the outcome (alignment) instead of announcing it.

**Alternatives, depending on tone**

- **Contoso: Where strategy meets architecture.** Cleanest if you want the alignment idea front and center.
- **Contoso: Map the enterprise. Align the business.** Slightly more action-oriented; "map" makes the visualization concrete.
- **Contoso: One picture of your enterprise. One plan everyone follows.** Longer, but it lands the collaboration angle harder.
- **Contoso: Enterprise architecture, seen clearly and governed together.** Closest to the original meaning if you need to keep the category name "enterprise architecture" for search or positioning.

**What I changed and why**

- Cut every adjective that describes the product rather than what the customer gets ("comprehensive," "integrated," "collaborative").
- Replaced abstract nouns with verbs. "Achieve strategic alignment through visualization" became "see" and "steer."
- Dropped "platform" and "capabilities." A tagline should not read like a product data sheet; that language belongs in the paragraph under the tagline.
- Kept it under ten words so it can sit on a slide, a website hero, or a booth banner.
