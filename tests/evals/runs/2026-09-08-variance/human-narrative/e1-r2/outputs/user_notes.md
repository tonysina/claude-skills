## Uncertainty

- **Cluster B is the closest call in the audit.** "Maria Chen felt the frustration in her chest" is the line most readers would point at as the AI tell, and it is the piece's only interior moment. I scored B as not-firing because the gate asks for the *dominant* mode and the skill supplies a hard 60%-of-emotional-beats rule; I counted three beats, one embodied (33%). Another auditor could reasonably count the beats differently — e.g. treating "drivers waited in the yard until the calls ended" as a fourth (behavioral) beat, which lowers the embodied share further, or discounting the two beats that sit inside Chen's quote as not narrator-controlled, which raises it to 1/1 and fires the cluster. The beat-counting unit is not defined in the skill. My call is defensible but not the only one.
- **Whether the case-study register row or the under-600-word row governs.** The input is 331 words, which fits the "<600 words" row (A, B, E, F only, no C1). I used the case-study row because the skill says "length is not the mode; register is." This only mattered for whether C1 was in scope, and C1 did not fire either way, so the outcome is unaffected.
- **The E/F collapse rule.** The skill scopes it to "the under-600-word row," which is arguably not this row. I checked it anyway and it does not apply, because its own exception ("unless A or B also fires") is triggered by A. Both readings give the same cluster count of 3.
- **The added reader-address line assumes an operator audience.** "If you have run a dispatch floor" is a claim about the reader's experience. If this asset targets finance or the C-suite rather than operations, the noun is wrong. I flagged this in the deliverable but the author has to make the call.

## Suggestions

- **Define the unit for cluster B's emotional-beat count.** The 60% rule is the only quantitative gate override in the skill and it is decisive here, but "emotional beat" is undefined. Does a feeling reported inside a customer quote count? Does a behavioral fact ("drivers waited in the yard") count? A one-line definition would make this reproducible across runs, which is exactly what `references/features.md` says the closed option sets are for.
- **The F fix has no defined behavior for non-fiction with no nameable referent.** The skill calls F "highest value-per-effort" and "a one-clause change," but in an anonymised or pre-approval customer story there may be no real name available at all. The C1 truth constraint ("if you don't know the fact, flag the intervention for the author instead of executing it") is the right rule and I applied it to F by analogy — but it is written under C1 only. Worth generalising it explicitly, or repeating it under F, since F is the cluster most likely to tempt fabrication.
- **Consider a note for the case-study register on what to do when G is the diagnosis.** "Too neat" in a customer story is very often uniformly positive moral polarity, and the register table bars the only fix for it. The skill handles this correctly by leaving it to the author, but an explicit sentence — "if the neatness is G and G is out of scope, say so and hand it back" — would stop an auditor from silently substituting a weaker intervention and calling the job done.
- **A worked non-fiction example would help.** The single worked example is thought leadership with 7 clusters firing and a full cap. A case study with a partial fire, a human-side gate that does not fire despite AI-side corroborators, and a blocked intervention would exercise the three rules that were hardest to apply here.
