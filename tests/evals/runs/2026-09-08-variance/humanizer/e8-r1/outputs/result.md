The programme began in 2017 as a small pilot in two boroughs. Over the next three years, it expanded to seven boroughs. The council increased its core funding in 2020, following positive results in an interim evaluation across all three measured domains.

Staff numbers grew from four to nineteen. The referral pathway was redesigned twice; the second redesign introduced a single point of access, which cut the average wait from eleven weeks to under four. That remains the programme's most-cited achievement.

The service now runs from three sites and receives about 4,000 referrals annually. Roughly two-thirds come from primary care, with the rest arriving through schools and self-referral. This balance has stayed stable since 2022.
